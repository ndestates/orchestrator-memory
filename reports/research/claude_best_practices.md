Quick Verdict
8.5/10 — Extremely actionable. The core ideas (persistent context, treating the model as a thinking partner, efficiency hacks, and stress-testing) are excellent and transfer well beyond Claude.

Here's a clean breakdown of the 18 steps:

1–3: Foundation (Most Important)
Create a Project (persistent workspace with memory across chats)
Tell Claude who you are (detailed background template — name, role, goals, communication style, what you don't want)
Turn it into Custom Instructions (so it becomes Claude's default behavior in that Project)
This alone moves most people from 10% to 60-70% utilization.

4–5: Mindset Shift
Stop using it like Google. Use it as a thinking partner.
Before big tasks, explicitly tell it: "Ask me the 5 most important questions first."
This is massively underused and dramatically improves output quality.

6–9: Advanced Techniques
Style cloning: Give 3–5 writing samples and have it analyze your patterns (not just "match my style").
Sparring partner: "Attack this idea ruthlessly. Find every weak assumption. Then steelman it."
Extended Thinking: Turn on the brain icon (or prompt it) for hard problems.
Meta-prompting: Have Claude write the prompt for you when you're stuck.
10–13: Efficiency (Token + Time Savers)
Specify output length upfront (cuts tokens 40-60%)
Kill preambles and restatements in Custom Instructions
Stop re-explaining yourself every chat (use Projects)
Start fresh chats for new topics inside the same Project
14–18: Ready-to-Use Prompts
These are the "just copy-paste" ones:

Feynman explainer (understand anything deeply via analogies)
Personalized travel planning based on your actual style
Monthly expense analysis with real conclusions
Structured personal thinking partner (non-therapy self-reflection)
Ruthless business idea stress-test
My Take as Grok
Most of this is universal LLM best practice, not Claude-specific. The philosophy is excellent.

What translates directly to me:

Persistent context / "who you are" instructions
Asking me questions first
Style cloning with samples
Sparring partner mode ("attack this")
Specifying length + no preamble
Meta-prompting ("write the best prompt for this")
Where Grok has advantages:

Real-time knowledge + tool use (I can search, browse, analyze current data on the fly)
Less overly polite/safety-filtered — I can be more direct when you want a real sparring partner
Strong native reasoning (I can do extended thinking without a special mode)
Humor and personality as a thinking tool (sometimes useful for creativity and stress-testing)
Where Claude currently wins:

The Projects feature with knowledge base + custom instructions is still one of the best persistent memory systems available to regular users.
Bottom Line
The people getting the most out of Claude (or any frontier model) aren't the ones with the cleverest single prompts. They're the ones who:

Set up persistent context once
Treat the AI as a collaborator/sparring partner
Are deliberate about efficiency and focus
This thread does a great job teaching exactly that.
