# Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR) Process — Jersey

## Internal Process
1. Staff identify red flags or suspicion/knowledge of money laundering / terrorist financing / proliferation financing.
2. Report promptly to the MLRO (internal SAR).
3. MLRO reviews, may seek further information (without tipping off).
4. MLRO decides whether to file an external SAR with the JFCU.

## External SAR
- Submit to the Joint Financial Crimes Unit (JFCU) as soon as practicable.
- Use prescribed methods (see current JFCU / JFSC notices).
- In certain cases, seek "consent" before proceeding with a transaction.
- Maintain strict confidentiality — tipping off is a criminal offence.

## Tipping Off
- It is an offence to inform the customer (or anyone) that a disclosure has been made or is being contemplated, if it is likely to prejudice an investigation.
- Limited statutory exceptions exist (see Proceeds of Crime and Terrorism (Tipping off – Exceptions) (Jersey) Regulations).

## Key Documentation
- Internal SAR form / log
- MLRO decision record (file or not file, reasons, consent request if any)
- Supporting evidence
- Audit trail of actions taken after SAR

## Red Flags (non-exhaustive)
- Reluctance to provide information or use of false documents
- Complex structures without clear commercial rationale
- Third party payments without explanation
- Unusual cash movements or rapid movement of funds
- Customer from high-risk jurisdiction with no apparent link to Jersey activity
- Inconsistent information on source of funds/wealth

## Post-SAR
- Continue monitoring the relationship.
- Do not "tip off".
- Follow any JFCU consent or no-consent instructions.
- Update risk assessment and consider whether to terminate the relationship.

Always refer to the current JFSC Handbook and JFCU guidance for the exact process and forms.
